Books

Programming Fundamentals & Best Practices

  • The Pragmatic Programmer: From Journeyman to Master
    Andrew Hunt, David Thomas

    Essential tips and best practices for software development, helping programmers enhance their problem-solving skills and deepen their craft. The Pragmatic Programmer is now something of a classic that implicitly reveals software development as both profession and lifestyle. It's essentially a philosophy of programming.

    Official Website

  • Pragmatic Thinking & Learning: Refactor Your Wetware
    Andy Hunt

    Delves into cognitive science and learning theory to offer techniques for improving programming skills. In many ways, this is the sequel to The Pragmatic Programmer, with more tips and tools to bring to programming.

    Official Website

  • The Secret Life of Programs
    Jonathan E. Steinhart

    A compendium of fundamental computer science concepts, clearly explained and well-organized. It explores the underlying ideas and technologies that make our programs work — the kind of knowledge we should already have, but often don't. As software developers, we can fall into the habit of thinking only in terms of programming languages, the operating system, and the immediate tools we use to create and run processes. This book expands that perspective in an enjoyable and accessible way.

    Book Review on YouTube

Functional Programming

  • Land of Lisp
    Conrad Barski, M.D.

    A playful, cartoon-filled introduction to Common Lisp by Conrad Barski. Instead of dry examples, it teaches programming through interactive games and quirky humor, making one of the oldest functional languages accessible and fun to learn.

    Publisher Page

  • Learn You a Haskell for Great Good!
    Miran Lipovaca

    A whimsical, illustrated guide to Haskell that blends humor with clear explanations. Beloved for its informal style, it introduces functional programming concepts like recursion, types, and monads in an approachable way, while retaining solid depth for serious learners.

    Official Website

  • The Little Schemer
    Daniel P. Friedman and Matthias Felleisen

    This book introduces basic metamathematical concepts that you play with on your computer using the Scheme programming language. You play with concepts—arithmetic, algebra, logic, recursion—and brush up against the limits of computability. It captures the essence of computing.

    This is the first of a series of related books.

  • The Art of Functional Programming
    Minh Quang Tran

    A clear and engaging introduction to the principles of functional programming, suitable for developers of any skill level. The book uses OCaml, Haskell, and Java to illustrate key concepts in a simple and accessible way, highlighting the elegance of this powerful paradigm. Of all the resources I've read, this is the best entry point into functional programming—it reshapes how you think about coding and problem solving.

    Official Website

AI Tools & Prompt Engineering

  • A Common-Sense Guide to AI Engineering
    Jay Wengrow

    AI engineering is a new topic and this book, still in beta, is one of the best, and one of the very few I've found so far. It surveys the landscape of modern AI tools and techniques, from embeddings and vector databases to prompt engineering and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG). Wengrow's trademark clear explanations make complex concepts accessible, helping developers understand not just how to use AI tools, but when and why to apply them. A practical guide for programmers ready to build with AI.

    Official Website

  • Process Over Magic: Beyond Vibe Coding
    Uberto Barbini

    This is another book on using AI as a tool. It's also in beta. Barbini warns against "vibe coding"—pointing your AI at a problem and letting it solve the problem without oversight, rather than treating AI as an assistant. The plan recommended here is to take small steps, get feedback, and stay in control. The book shows how to build effective guardrails, craft prompts that work, and collaborate with AI in ways that feel natural and genuinely productive. Companion videos demonstrate these practices in action. As Barbini puts it: how do the best developers amplify their abilities with AI? It's not magic—it's process.

    Official Website

Mathematics for Programmers

  • Good Math
    Mark C. Chu-Carroll

    In Good Math, you'll explore number theory, logic, proofs, truth trees, and Turing machines—all explained with clarity and engaging examples. The book touches on many aspects of metamathematics and shows how code can be used to explore/play with abstract ideas. Even in topics you're already familiar with, it offers fresh insights that make mathematics feel both practical and inspiring.

    Official Blog Website

  • Math for Programming
    Ronald T. Kneusel

    This book offers 16 comprehensive chapters on topics every programmer should know. It's not only a solid review of mathematics in general, but also a practical guide to applying it in code. The explanations are concise yet thorough, and the Python examples make it hands-on rather than just theory. That's the real selling point for me. I like pairing this book with The Secret Life of Programs as go-to references—they complement each other perfectly.

    Publisher Page

  • Burn Math Class
    Jason Wilkes

    Wilkes reimagines what we have been exposed to in conventional math education, reconstructing core concepts from the ground up. He focuses on mathematical notation and how to reinvent it to gain more insight into what it should represent. For those who love math but hated how it was taught. The author says of math, "We have been teaching the subject backwards."

    On Google Books

Theory, Logic & Philosophy

  • Gödel, Escher, Bach: An Eternal Golden Braid
    Douglas R. Hofstadter

    The book that sparked my interest in programming. Written by cognitive scientist Douglas Hofstadter, it asks "how does a self come out of things that have no self"—a question posed before philosopher David Chalmers coined the term "Hard Problem" of consciousness. The topic is beautifully explored here in terms of metamathematics and in terms of reductionism and scientific materialism. While I don't share this perspective, the book's alternative angle proved illuminating.

    MIT Lecture on Gödel, Escher, Bach

  • International Journal of Mathematics and Consciousness
    Journal

    This journal explores the intersection of mathematics and human consciousness, addressing one of science's most profound questions: how subjective awareness arises and operates. It provides a forum for mathematicians, scientists, and thinkers to present rigorous theories that link consciousness with physical and abstract phenomena. Emphasizing strict logical reasoning while encouraging creativity, the journal welcomes both new proposals and well-founded counterarguments. By framing consciousness through the precision of mathematics, it seeks to illuminate how awareness shapes thought, perception, and our understanding of reality.

    Journal Archive

  • Laws of Form
    G. Spencer-Brown

    An influential text introducing a unique symbolic system that explores the foundations of logic and mathematics. Spencer-Brown develops a calculus based on the concept of distinction—the fundamental act of drawing a boundary. From this simple starting point, he reconstructs Boolean algebra and proposes connections to fundamental questions in mathematics, logic, and even consciousness. The book's unconventional style and profound implications have made it a cult classic among mathematicians, philosophers, and computer scientists.
